<section id="overview">
<h2>What the Bair Hugger lawsuit is about</h2>
<p>Bair Hugger lawsuits allege that forced-air warming systems used during surgery contributed to deep surgical-site or periprosthetic joint infections and that defendants did not adequately address the alleged risk. 3M and Arizant dispute causation and maintain that the systems are safe and effective. The <a href="https://www.mnd.uscourts.gov/node/532">District of Minnesota's MDL description</a> presents both sides.</p>
<p>These are disputed product-liability allegations. An infection after surgery does not itself establish that a Bair Hugger device caused the infection.</p>
</section>

<h2>Verified Bair Hugger litigation updates</h2>
<h3>September 1, 2026: 8,433 actions pending</h3>
<p>The <a href="https://www.jpml.uscourts.gov/sites/jpml/files/Pending_MDL_Dockets_By_Actions_Pending-September-1-2026.pdf">JPML report</a> lists 8,433 pending actions and 10,739 historical actions. On August 3, the corresponding figures were 8,407 and 10,716.</p>
<p><strong>For readers:</strong> Pending actions are not approved claims, and the historical total is not a claimant or payment count.</p>
<h3>2025–2026: plaintiff fact-sheet enforcement</h3>
<p>A <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-mnd-0_15-md-02666/pdf/USCOURTS-mnd-0_15-md-02666-26.pdf">January 2026 report and recommendation</a> describes the court's process for identifying cases with missing verified plaintiff fact sheets, issuing orders to show cause, and recommending dismissal when a plaintiff does not comply.</p>
<p><strong>For readers:</strong> This is claim administration, not a finding that every case is valid or invalid and not a settlement announcement.</p>
<h3>2021: appellate court restored the general-causation dispute</h3>
<p>The <a href="https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/21/08/192899P.pdf">Eighth Circuit opinion</a> reversed the MDL court's exclusion of plaintiffs' general-causation experts and the related summary judgment on the record before it. The court described its admissibility conclusion as narrow.</p>
<p><strong>For readers:</strong> Allowing expert opinions to be heard is not a ruling that a warming system caused any person's infection.</p>

<section id="device-and-injury-scope">
<span id="key-issues"></span>
<h2>Device and injury questions that matter</h2>
<p>The litigation concerns Bair Hugger forced-air warming products used to maintain body temperature during surgery. A chart may say that warming was used without naming the system, so facility and anesthesia records can matter.</p>
<p>Claims commonly focus on deep infections following orthopedic implant procedures. Superficial irritation, an unrelated infection, or a procedure with no documented forced-air warming use presents a different record. The exact surgery, device, organism, infection location, diagnosis timing, and treatment history all require review.</p>
</section>

<section id="claim-review">
<span id="eligibility"></span><span id="claim-evaluation"></span><span id="harder-claims"></span>
<h2>Who may have a claim to review?</h2>
<p>A useful starting file documents a surgery in which Bair Hugger equipment was used, a later deep surgical-site or joint infection, and the treatment that followed. A lawyer and qualified medical experts still must examine causation, product identification, other infection risks, warnings, applicable law, and timing.</p>
<p>Do not omit earlier infections, wound problems, immune conditions, smoking history, diabetes, repeat surgeries, or other facts that may affect the analysis. Complete records are more useful than a timeline built only from favorable details.</p>
</section>

<section id="settlement-status">
<h2>Bair Hugger settlement status</h2>
<p>The <a href="https://www.mnd.uscourts.gov/mdl-bair-hugger">official MDL page</a> and reviewed court records do not announce a global settlement program, claimant matrix, or payout schedule. Dismissals for missing records and outcomes in individual cases do not create a settlement for other plaintiffs.</p>
</section>

<section id="records">
<h2>Records to organize for a Bair Hugger review</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Index surgery:</strong> operative, anesthesia, circulating-nurse, temperature-management, and implant records.</li>
<li><strong>Product identification:</strong> chart entries, supply or equipment logs, and facility responses identifying the warming system.</li>
<li><strong>Infection:</strong> cultures, imaging, laboratory results, infectious-disease notes, and diagnosis dates.</li>
<li><strong>Treatment:</strong> debridement, revision, implant removal or replacement, antibiotic, rehabilitation, and follow-up records.</li>
<li><strong>Costs and impact:</strong> bills, insurance statements, time away from work, and documented later care.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use the <a href="/lawsuits/bair-hugger/infection-records/">Bair Hugger infection-records guide</a> to separate documented facts, missing records, and questions for counsel.</p>
</section>

<section id="bair-hugger-deadlines">
<span id="state-law"></span>
<h2>What is the deadline to file?</h2>
<p>There is no single national filing deadline for every Bair Hugger claim. State statutes of limitation and repose, the surgery and diagnosis timeline, discovery facts, and prior filings can affect the time available. MDL fact-sheet deadlines apply to particular filed cases and are not substitutes for a personal deadline analysis.</p>
</section>
