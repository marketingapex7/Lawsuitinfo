<section id="overview">
<h2>What the NEC baby formula litigation is about</h2>
<p>Families allege that certain cow's-milk-based preterm infant formulas and fortifiers made by Abbott or Mead Johnson increased the risk of necrotizing enterocolitis in premature infants and that warnings were inadequate. The defendants dispute defect, causation, and liability.</p>
<p>This page does not give infant-feeding advice. A product exposure followed by NEC does not itself prove that the product caused the disease or that a legal claim is valid.</p>
</section>

<section id="litigation-updates">
<h2>Verified NEC litigation updates</h2>
<h3>September 1, 2026: 847 actions pending</h3>
<p>The <a href="https://www.jpml.uscourts.gov/sites/jpml/files/Pending_MDL_Dockets_By_Actions_Pending-September-1-2026.pdf">JPML report</a> lists 847 pending and 1,057 historical actions. The August figures were 825 and 1,035.</p>
<h3>July 24, 2026: Abbott bellwether judgment affirmed</h3>
<p>In <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca7-25-02587/pdf/USCOURTS-ca7-25-02587-0.pdf">Mar v. Abbott Laboratories</a>, the Seventh Circuit affirmed summary judgment because the evidence did not show that the proposed alternative warning would have changed the feeding decision and prevented the infant's death. The court expressly did not decide the broader product-causation issue Abbott asked it to reach.</p>
<h3>July 28, 2026: five Mead cases returned for further proceedings</h3>
<p>The <a href="https://media.ca7.uscourts.gov/cgi-bin/OpinionsWeb/processWebInputExternal.pl?Path=Y2026%2FD07-28%2FC%3A25-2324%3AJ%3ARipple%3Aaut%3AT%3AfnOp%3AN%3A3581735%3AS%3A0&Submit=Display">Seventh Circuit</a> reversed the district court's fraudulent-joinder approach in five cases. That decision concerned federal jurisdiction and did not find that either manufacturer's product was defective or caused NEC.</p>
</section>

<section id="product-scope">
<span id="key-issues"></span>
<h2>Premature-infant product and manufacturer scope</h2>
<p>The litigation focuses on specified cow's-milk-based preterm nutrition products and fortifiers used in NICUs. The record must identify the manufacturer and product actually administered. Similac products are associated with Abbott; Enfamil products are associated with Mead Johnson. A chart entry such as “formula” or “fortifier” alone may be insufficient.</p>
<p>Term-infant products, retail recalls for contamination, donor milk, and hospital feeding policy can involve different facts or proceedings. Preserve the exact order, administration entry, product name, lot if recorded, and source facility.</p>
</section>

<section id="injury-treatment">
<h2>NEC diagnosis and treatment records</h2>
<p>NEC is a serious disease seen particularly in premature infants. A legal record review should keep gestational age, birth weight, prematurity complications, feeding changes, first symptoms, diagnosis and staging, imaging, laboratory findings, antibiotics, surgery, pathology, and later care in chronological order.</p>
<p>Prematurity and other medical factors matter. A diagnosis after a feed does not settle medical causation, and this guide does not recommend or discourage any feeding plan.</p>
</section>

<section id="trial-appeal-boundaries">
<h2>How to read trials, verdicts, and appeals</h2>
<p>A federal bellwether decision applies to the evidence and governing law in that selected case. A state-court verdict arises outside the federal MDL and may face post-trial motions or appeal. A jurisdiction ruling decides where a case proceeds, not whether the product caused an injury.</p>
<p>Do not use a headline verdict, dismissal, or appellate ruling as a payout estimate or as proof of another family's claim.</p>
</section>

<section id="claim-review">
<span id="eligibility"></span><span id="claim-evaluation"></span><span id="harder-claims"></span>
<h2>Factors in an individual claim review</h2>
<p>A useful starting file identifies prematurity and birth details, every feed and fortifier, the specific manufacturer and product, the NEC course, and resulting treatment. Counsel must assess warnings, causation, alternative risks, hospital decisions, damages, applicable law, and filing time.</p>
<p>Include maternal milk and donor milk records, parenteral nutrition, feeding interruptions, infections, congenital conditions, transfusions, medications, and all other facts documented in the chart.</p>
</section>

<section id="settlement-status">
<h2>NEC baby formula settlement status</h2>
<p>Reviewed official federal records do not announce a global MDL settlement program, claimant matrix, or universal payment deadline. Individual state or federal outcomes do not create a standard settlement amount for other families.</p>
</section>

<section id="records">
<h2>NICU and feeding records to organize</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Birth:</strong> gestational age, birth weight, delivery, transfer, and prematurity diagnoses.</li>
<li><strong>Feeds:</strong> orders, administration records, flowsheets, product and fortifier names, volumes, routes, changes, and lot information if recorded.</li>
<li><strong>NEC:</strong> symptom notes, staging, radiology and images, laboratory results, cultures, and consultations.</li>
<li><strong>Treatment:</strong> bowel rest, antibiotics, surgery, pathology, ostomy and reversal, nutrition, discharge, and later care.</li>
<li><strong>Impact:</strong> bills, insurance records, travel, family leave, home care, and long-term treatment.</li>
</ul>
<p>Use the <a href="/lawsuits/nec-baby-formula/nicu-feeding-records/">NEC NICU and feeding-records guide</a> to build a neutral chronology from the complete chart.</p>
</section>

<section id="nec-baby-formula-deadlines">
<span id="state-law"></span>
<h2>What is the deadline to file?</h2>
<p>There is no single national deadline. State statutes, a child's age, representative or estate issues, diagnosis and discovery facts, prior filings, and court orders may affect timing. MDL deadlines for an existing case do not calculate a new family's filing period.</p>
</section>
