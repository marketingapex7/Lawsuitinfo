<section id="overview">
<h2>What the lawsuit is about</h2>
<p><strong>The Bard PowerPort lawsuit — also written "power port" and searched as the port catheter lawsuit — alleges that implanted port catheter devices caused complications such as catheter fracture, migration, infection, thrombosis, embolization, or removal surgery, and that warnings were inadequate.</strong> Federal cases are coordinated in MDL-3081 before Senior Judge David G. Campbell in the U.S. District Court for the District of Arizona. Defendants dispute the allegations.</p>
<p>As of November 2, 2026, the JPML reported <strong>4,345 pending actions</strong> in MDL-3081. This guide explains status, evidence, deadlines, and common claim-review issues in plain English.</p>
</section>

<section id="latest-update">
<h2>Bard PowerPort Lawsuit update</h2>
<p><strong>CMO 52 now establishes a court-directed settlement and remand process.</strong> The order says the November 13 conference addressed a proposed settlement process and what will happen to cases that do not settle. It does not announce a global settlement agreement, settlement fund, claimant eligibility grid, or individual payout amount. <a href="https://www.azd.uscourts.gov/sites/azd/files/23-03081-12139.pdf">Read CMO 52</a>.</p>
<p>New cases may be transferred to or directly filed in MDL-3081 through <strong>February 28, 2027</strong>. The parties then have until <strong>September 1, 2027</strong> to attempt to negotiate settlement of cases pending in the MDL. Cases with an executed release or term sheet by that date may remain until <strong>March 1, 2028</strong> to complete settlement paperwork and file a stipulated dismissal. Other cases are to be recommended for remand or transferred under 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a). These court-management dates do not replace an individual statute-of-limitations analysis.</p>
<p>The Hicks docket records October 15–21 expert rulings, the October 26 denial of summary judgment, and an affirmative-defense ruling filed November 4. The September 4 Divelbliss verdict awarded $40 million in compensatory damages and no punitive damages. <a href="https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USCOURTS-azd-2_23-md-03081/USCOURTS-azd-2_23-md-03081-43/context">Federal docket and orders</a> · <a href="https://www.aboutlawsuits.com/wp-content/uploads/2026-09-04-Verdict.pdf">Filed verdict</a>.</p>
<p>The <a href="https://www.jpml.uscourts.gov/sites/jpml/files/Pending_MDL_Dockets_By_Actions_Pending-November-2-2026.pdf">November 2, 2026 JPML report</a> lists <strong>4,345 pending actions</strong> and 4,636 total historical actions. The figures are docket counts, not settlement eligibility or payment counts.</p>
<p><strong>Settlement status:</strong> CMO 52 establishes the process and deadlines described above, but the reviewed public order does not provide universal settlement terms or a guaranteed result for any individual claim.</p>

<section id="qualification">
<h2>Who may qualify for a Bard PowerPort lawsuit?</h2>
<p>There is no automatic qualification rule. A claim review may consider whether a Bard implanted port catheter device was used, whether records identify the device, what complication occurred, when symptoms began, and whether the person required treatment, revision, or removal.</p>
<p>Commonly reviewed complications may include catheter fracture, device migration, infection, blood clots, embolization, vascular injury, or surgery to remove or replace the device. Medical proof matters because symptoms alone usually do not identify the device or establish causation.</p>
</section>

<section id="evidence">
<h2>What proof may help a Bard PowerPort claim?</h2>
<p>Useful records may include implant records, device stickers, hospital supply records, operative reports, imaging studies, emergency records, infection records, clotting records, and removal or revision surgery reports. A reviewer may also ask for the name of the implanting facility and the treating doctors.</p>
<p>If the port has been removed, pathology, explant, or device-retention information may matter. If the port remains implanted, imaging and current treatment records may help document the alleged problem.</p>
</section>

<section id="deadlines">
<h2>Bard PowerPort lawsuit deadline considerations</h2>
<p>Deadlines vary by state and may turn on implant date, complication date, when the person learned the device may be involved, and whether the claim is a personal injury, survival, or wrongful-death matter. Device cases can be complicated because an implant may remain in place for years before a complication is identified.</p>
<p>Anyone researching a possible claim should gather implant date, complication date, removal date if any, diagnosis or imaging dates, and any communications about device failure or suspected causation.</p>
</section>
