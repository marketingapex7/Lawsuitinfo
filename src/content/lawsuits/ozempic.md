<section id="overview">
<h2>What the Ozempic / GLP-1 lawsuit is about</h2>
<p>Ozempic and other GLP-1 lawsuits allege inadequate warnings about serious gastrointestinal injuries. MDL-3094 coordinates those federal cases before Judge Karen Spencer Marston in Pennsylvania. Defendants dispute the allegations. NAION vision-loss claims proceed separately in MDL-3163.</p>
</section>

<section id="ozempic-drugs-included">
<h2>Which drugs are included?</h2>
<p>Filed cases involve GLP-1 receptor agonists from two manufacturers:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Novo Nordisk:</strong> Ozempic and Rybelsus (semaglutide for type 2 diabetes), Wegovy (semaglutide for weight management), and Saxenda (liraglutide).</li>
<li><strong>Eli Lilly:</strong> Mounjaro and Zepbound (tirzepatide) and Trulicity (dulaglutide).</li>
</ul>
<p>Both diabetes patients and weight-loss patients appear among filed cases. The medication involved, dose, duration of use, and documented injury all factor into claim review.</p>
</section>

<section id="ozempic-qualifications">
<h2>Who may qualify for the Ozempic lawsuit?</h2>
<p>There is no automatic qualification rule. Claim review commonly considers:</p>
<ul>
<li>Documented use of a GLP-1 medication, supported by prescription and pharmacy records.</li>
<li>A diagnosis such as gastroparesis, ileus, intestinal pseudo-obstruction, or bowel obstruction — often supported by a gastric emptying study, endoscopy, imaging, or hospitalization records.</li>
<li>Injury timing relative to medication use, and whether other causes were ruled out or disputed.</li>
<li>State filing deadlines and individual procedural facts.</li>
</ul>
<p>Severe, medically documented injuries — particularly those involving hospitalization — have been the focus of filed cases.</p>
</section>

<section id="ozempic-evidence">
<h2>What evidence can support an Ozempic lawsuit review?</h2>
<p><strong>A useful review usually starts with records that identify the GLP-1 medication, show when and how long it was used, document the diagnosed injury, and establish the timing between use, symptoms, and treatment.</strong> The evidence needed depends on the alleged injury and individual medical history; possessing a particular record does not guarantee that a claim qualifies.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Medication proof:</strong> prescription records, pharmacy fills, product name, dose changes, and dates of use.</li>
<li><strong>Diagnosis proof:</strong> gastroenterology records, discharge summaries, and records identifying gastroparesis, ileus, bowel obstruction, or another alleged injury.</li>
<li><strong>Testing and treatment:</strong> gastric-emptying studies, imaging, endoscopy, emergency care, hospitalization, surgery, and follow-up treatment when relevant.</li>
<li><strong>Timeline:</strong> dates of medication use, first symptoms, diagnosis, treatment, and any decision to stop or change medication under medical supervision.</li>
<li><strong>Other medical context:</strong> records concerning alternative explanations, prior gastrointestinal history, and other medications may also matter.</li>
</ul>
<p>The federal proceeding's current schedule and orders are available from the <a href="https://www.paed.uscourts.gov/mdl/mdl3094" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Eastern District of Pennsylvania's MDL-3094 page</a>. Court coordination does not create a universal qualification checklist, and state filing deadlines still require individual review.</p>
</section>

<section id="eligibility">
<h2>Who may be affected</h2>
<p>People who used a GLP-1 medication and then experienced severe, documented gastrointestinal injury — especially gastroparesis confirmed by a gastric emptying study, or a bowel obstruction or ileus requiring emergency care — are the focus of current filings. Family members may have wrongful-death-related questions where an injury contributed to a death; those claims involve additional state-specific rules.</p>
</section>

<section id="ozempic-injury-details">
<h2>Injuries alleged in filed cases</h2>
<p><strong>Gastroparesis</strong> — delayed gastric emptying that can cause persistent vomiting, malnutrition, and long-term complications. <strong>Ileus and bowel obstruction</strong> — intestinal blockage or paralysis that can require hospitalization or surgery. <strong>NAION</strong> — a sudden optic-nerve injury associated in some studies with semaglutide use, capable of causing permanent vision loss; NAION claims are consolidated in their own proceeding, MDL-3163 (216 pending actions as of September 1, 2026). Filed complaints also describe severe dehydration, aspiration events, and related complications.</p>
</section>
