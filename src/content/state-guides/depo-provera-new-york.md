<section id="state-overview">
<h2>What New York residents should know</h2>
<p>New York residents have two possible litigation settings. Some cases are proceeding in New York state court, while federal cases are centralized in <strong>MDL-3140</strong> before Judge M. Casey Rodgers in the Northern District of Florida. Each plaintiff keeps an individual claim.</p>
<p>The litigation alleges that Depo-Provera can cause intracranial meningioma and that warnings were inadequate. Pfizer and the Pharmacia entities dispute the allegations.</p>
</section>

<section id="state-depo-provera-context">
<h2>How are New York and federal Depo-Provera cases coordinated?</h2>
<p>Federal Case Management Order No. 11 states that the March 6, 2026 case-management conference was held jointly with New York Justice Sabrina Kraus and Delaware Judge Kathleen Vavala. The order reported 95 New York state cases involving 104 plaintiffs at that time. Joint court management is intended to reduce conflicting schedules and duplicative work; it does not combine all plaintiffs into a class action or decide whether any claim will succeed.</p>

<section id="eligibility">
<h2>What facts are commonly reviewed in a New York Depo-Provera claim?</h2>
<p>There is no automatic qualification rule. A review generally compares documented medication use with the diagnosis and filing timeline.</p>
<ul>
<li>Injection, prescription, clinic, pharmacy, and insurance records identifying Depo-Provera and dates of use.</li>
<li>MRI or other brain-imaging reports, neurology notes, pathology, surgery, radiation, and monitoring records.</li>
<li>The timing of use, symptoms, diagnosis, and treatment.</li>
<li>Facts that determine which court and filing-deadline rules apply.</li>
</ul>
